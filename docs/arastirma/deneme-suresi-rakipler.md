# Free-trial length in comparable consumer subscription apps (checked 18 November 2026)

Purpose: evidence for choosing between a 7-day and a 14-day free trial for a parent-facing family subscription app (cardless trial at sign-up, limited free plan afterwards, Turkey first).

## How to read this report

- **"Opened = yes"** means I downloaded the page on 18 Nov 2026 and read the wording myself. In practice that is the Apple App Store listing (US and Turkish storefront: description text, developer replies, in-app purchase list) and one competitor's public website.
- **"Opened = snippet"** means I only saw the text a web search returned for that page. It is weaker evidence, even when the page is the company's own help centre.
- **The session's network allowlist blocked almost every company website, help centre and Google Play** (see Limitations). So trial *length* is well evidenced where the App Store listing states it, and less well evidenced where it does not.
- App Store in-app purchase (IAP) lists show up to ten price points, often unlabelled and mixing current and legacy products. Prices from them are indicative, not a price sheet.
- No currency conversion was done. TRY figures are what the Turkish App Store listing showed.

**The other 13 apps**

**Card-required versus cardless (32 apps that have a trial)**

- Payment method required and auto-renewing into a paid plan: 27. Stated in the listing or help page for most; implied by standard App Store auto-renew terms for a few (Routinery, Levelty, BusyKid, HOMER).
- Clearly cardless, no purchase needed: 2 as the main offer (S'moresUp 45 days, YNAB 34 days on the web). Two more as a secondary offer: Finch's 3-day Plus trial and Strava's 30-day "Subscription Preview".
- Wording suggests cardless but does not say so outright: 3 (Brili 10 days, Lovevery 28 days, Kidslox 3 days).

**What the direct competitors do**

- **Joon** (closest product): 7 days, App Store trial that renews into a paid plan, with a free version to fall back on. US$12.99 a month or US$89.99 a year.
- **S'moresUp** (closest *trial model*: no purchase, drops to a free plan): 45 days, or 450 completed chores, whichever comes first.
- **Brili**: 10 days, then choose a plan. **Tiimo**: 7 days, yearly plan only (it was 14 days in 2021). **Finch**: 3 days cardless plus 7 days converting.
- Family organisers run longer: **FamilyWall** 30 days, **Cozi Gold** 14 days (web sign-up only).
- Kids-money apps all give about one month (Greenlight, BusyKid, Acorns Early).
- **Habitica** has no trial; it is plain freemium.

**Three observations for the 7-versus-14 decision**

1. Seven days is the norm for card-required App Store trials (13 of 29). Fourteen days is rare in this set (2 of 29); apps that go beyond 7 mostly go to about a month (11 of 29).
2. Every app found with the same model as ours (no card, falls back to a free plan or stops) runs longer than 7 days: S'moresUp 45, YNAB 34, Strava preview 30, Lovevery 28, Brili 10. The only short cardless example is Finch's 3-day teaser, which sits beside a 7-day card trial. The sample is small (five to six apps).
3. No Turkish kids or parenting app in the set advertises a 14-day trial. The two with a verifiable trial (Otsimo, MentalUP) use 7 days.

- **Yearly plan only:** Tiimo, Otsimo (Turkish listing). **Monthly plan only:** Reading Eggs, ABCmouse (web).

## 5. Why they chose or changed a trial length

All of these are from search snippets unless marked "opened".

1. **Duolingo, 14 to 7 (end of 2024).** On RevenueCat's Sub Club podcast (2 Mar 2026), Duolingo's Anmol Tiwari said the company ran a two-week trial until about the end of 2024, then tested seven days; the main gain was that conversion results arrive in 7 days instead of 14, which "doubled Duolingo's experimentation velocity" [S17].
2. **Duolingo, 7 to one month (2026).** On the Q1 2026 earnings call (4 May 2026) CEO Luis von Ahn said: "Historically, Duolingo has given a 7-day free trial, and that has worked well for us," and that longer trials bring more bookings. On the Q2 call he said the company is shifting most free trials to one month because significantly more people take the trial and more convert to payers. Caveat reported alongside: longer trials delay when users become paying subscribers [S17]. The App Store listing still says 14 days (opened) [26].
3. **YNAB, 34 days.** YNAB's site explains that most trials are 30 days but many months have 31, so 34 days lets a user live through a full month and its rollover. No card is needed [S15].
4. **Tiimo, 14 to 7.** A developer reply of 30 Jun 2021 says "a subscription based app with a 14-day free trial"; the current listing says 7 days, yearly plan only (both opened) [5]. No reason given.
5. **Brili, 30 to 10.** An older developer reply (snippet, undated) says "we offer a 30 day free trial to all our users"; the current listing says 10 days (opened) [4]. No reason given.
6. **Pok Pok.** Per RevenueCat's write-up on kids apps, the founders put the paywall at the start and offer the trial "so the kid can try the app and see if it's a good fit" [S26].
7. **Reading.com / Teaching.com.** Hiding the free trial on monthly plans to push annual reportedly cut lifetime value by 17%, because hesitant users need an on-ramp; the company later swapped trials for guarantees [S26].
8. **Industry benchmark (not a company statement).** RevenueCat's State of Subscription Apps 2026 reports median trial conversion of 42.5% for trials of 17 days or more against 25.5% for four days or fewer; on annual plans 24% (four days or fewer), 33% (5 to 9), 43% (10 to 16), 45% (17 to 32). It adds that no single length leads on every measure [S24]. These figures describe card-required store trials, not cardless ones.

**"We'll remind you before the trial ends"**

- **S'moresUp** help centre: "As you get closer to the end of your trial period, you will be notified through the app and/or email" [S18].
- **Joon**: an App Store reviewer wrote that the paywall "specifically said they'd send reminders about the free trial ending" and that none arrived [S20].
- **Duolingo**: a deal site notes the Super page "says it'll remind you before the trial ends" [S17].
- **Origin of the pattern (Blinkist, 2020; not on our list)**: a timeline paywall with a reminder two days before the end reportedly raised trial starts 23% and notification opt-in from 6% to 74%. Replication is mixed: one app saw trial starts up 4% but trial-to-paid down 7.8%, and Flo reported it did not work for them [S25].
- I could not see any app's actual paywall screen, so I cannot confirm which of the listed apps show a day-by-day timeline today.

## 6. Limitations
